\begin{abstract}
% Author rewrite (2026-11-27), corrected against the evidence. Pointers: EV = evidence/README.md. Rows S1, C1b, C3d, M2a, M1d.
We describe four attacks on limit-order traders in our fully on-chain decentralized exchange. % EV E1 to E4
They exploit the contract's execution limits and matching rules, guided, we argue, by public order data, and each reproduces in local Foundry tests. % EV E1 to E4, E8, E9
In one, an adversary places ten one-lot (dust) orders to exhaust a buyer's ten-fill budget; if the follow-up trade lands before anyone clears the book, a buyer whose limit is 33 percent above the ask pays 5.0 quote more on a 15.0 trade. % EV E1 33% row; E9 keeper
In a second, a one-lot self-trade fires a stop into the adversary's bid, and in a third the adversary takes half a stop's proceeds in our test, in one transaction that no keeper can interrupt once the adversary triggers it first. % EV E2, E9 atomic
A fourth, a wall of 101 dust bids, blocks new non-crossing bids but costs 8.4 times the gas needed to clear it. % EV E4; COST_MODEL griefing table
In a research copy, canceling a still-crossed remainder removes the sandwich and snipe profits but, used alone, turns theft into an extrapolated billion-round delay and cancels the sniped stop; a 0.1-base minimum bounds the delay to 10 rounds. % EV E5, E7, E10
\end{abstract}
