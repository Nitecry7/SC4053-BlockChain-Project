\begin{abstract}
% Stage 4 revision. Pointers: EV = evidence/README.md. Rows S1, C1b, C3d, M2a, M1d.
Correct bookkeeping does not protect a decentralized exchange's traders from whoever controls transaction order.
We study this in our fully on-chain limit order book, with gas-bounded loops and public orders, and reproduce four attacks in local Foundry tests. % EV E1 to E4, E8, E9
Ten one-lot (dust) orders exhaust a buyer's ten-fill budget; if the attacker's follow-up lands before anyone clears the book, a buyer whose limit is 33\% above the ask pays 5.0 extra quote on a 15.0 trade. % EV E1 33% row; E9 keeper
A one-lot self-trade fires a stop order, and a stop can be sniped for half its proceeds in our test, in one transaction that no keeper can interrupt once the attacker triggers it first. % EV E2, E9 atomic
A wall of 101 dust bids blocks new non-crossing bids but costs the attacker 8.4 times the gas a defender needs to clear it. % EV E4; COST_MODEL griefing table
In a research copy of the engine, canceling a still-crossed remainder removes the sandwich and snipe profits but, used alone, turns theft into an extrapolated $10^{9}$-round delay and cancels the sniped stop; a 0.1-base minimum bounds the delay to 10 rounds. % EV E5, E7, E10
We recommend both, plus redesigned stop activation.
\end{abstract}
