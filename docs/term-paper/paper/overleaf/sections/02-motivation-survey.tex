\subsection{Why Security over Privacy and Scalability}\label{sec:motivation}
Due to the public nature of the Ethereum blockchain, users can be de-anonymized: combining two embeddings of an address's position in the transaction graph reduces a user's anonymity set by a factor of about three~\cite{src25beres2021}. % [25] p. 7 (F4, 3.03x); EM S8
Privacy is important because every limit and stop order is public and public Ethereum activity allows addresses to be linked. % IOrderBook.sol:49
Scalability is important because implementing an order book on chain is expensive.
In the worst case scenario, with each order potentially tradeable, the Lissy call market could only execute 38 to 152 trades within a single block using the July 2020 gas limit~\cite{src09moosavi2023}. % [09] p. 6 to 7 Table 5, p. 4; EM M5e
Using a testnet of Arbitrum, which is a layer 2 rollup solution, a call market was implemented to test order book closures; compared to closing the market on layer one, it reduced layer-one gas costs by 99.88\%, not counting layer-two work~\cite{src09moosavi2023}. % [09] p. 7 Table 6, p. 8 (F6); EM S3
Despite the absence of a public mempool (the pool of pending transactions), the rollups studied still carry extractable value; the primary sources measured were arbitrages and liquidations~\cite{src23torres2024}. % [23] p. 2, p. 4, p. 7 (F2 to F4); EM S4, M11f
Security concerns come before the other two.
Three of the four attacks described herein exploit loop bounds that we introduced to save gas, and we also argue that the public order book and stop list give an attacker the best guesses as to where an attack should be aimed. % OB:20-21; EM S1, S6
Qin et al.\ claim that extractable value is likely to remain as long as executions are transparent and one side has the choice to select the order~\cite{src05qin2022}. % [05] p. 13 (F4); EM S5

\subsection{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\emph{Front running and extractable value.}
Eskandari et al.\ classify front running attacks as displacement, insertion and suppression, and claim that fully on-chain order books are vulnerable to the first two types of attacks~\cite{src02eskandari2019}. % [02] p. 4 to 5, p. 7 (F1, F3)
Daian et al.\ observed that in the proof-of-work Ethereum network, prior to builder markets, bots bid up gas prices in priority gas auctions to buy an early position in the transaction order~\cite{src01daian2020}. % [01] p. 1, p. 5 to 6 (F1); EM C1e
Zhou et al.\ state that their work is the first to study sandwich attacks in a quantitative and formal manner, and they do not include limit order books in their study~\cite{src04zhou2021}. % [04] p. 1 (F2)
Qin et al.\ found 750{,}529 sandwich attacks on Uniswap, SushiSwap and Bancor, which have earned attackers USD 174.34 million~\cite{src05qin2022}. % [05] p. 4 (F2); EM C1d
On an AMM, a sandwich attack moves the pool's reserves before a trade is executed and restores them after~\cite{src04zhou2021}; in a limit order book, we argue, front running attacks involve trading against the order of the victim. % [04] F1, F10; reasoning
The sandwich attacks on AMMs show that front running is a profitable attack, although its magnitude on an order book held in a contract is unknown. % EM C1d caveat

\emph{Economic security.}
Babel et al.\ use the term `extractable value' to measure the economic security of composed contracts and show that composing contracts can increase it; they also provide a formal definition for `extractable value'~\cite{src08babel2023}. % [08] p. 5, p. 16 (F1, F5, F6); EM S9
We argue that their definition applies, by analogy, to a single contract, where rules can appear safe to implement on their own, but attacks can still occur from combining them. % reasoning
Zhou et al.\ document 28 incidents of on-chain oracle manipulation attacks on decentralized finance protocols in Ethereum and the BNB Smart Chain between April 2018 and April 2022, representing 15\% of all incidents, and they are the most frequent type at the protocol layer~\cite{src06zhou2023}. % [06] p. 1, p. 12 (F3, F4); EM S10
Academic papers on price oracle attacks are rare, and our stop trigger is an example of such a price oracle. % [06] p. 1 ("few academic papers")

\emph{Order books on chain and batch auctions.}
Lissy determines the cost of an on-chain book with respect to gas and suggests that a call market makes basic insertion and displacement attacks ineffective, because time priority is lost in a call~\cite{src09moosavi2023}. % [09] p. 4 to 7, p. 8 Table 7 (F7); EM M5b
Lissy's system is the most closely related to ours, but in our system orders are matched on arrival with the best price first, so the order of transaction arrival determines who trades with whom, and who is the maker. % OB:38-40, 67-68, 115, 276
This allows for the fill-cap sandwiches and stop snipes described in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
However, in Lissy, shuffling orders within a call gives the same result. % [09] p. 8 (F7)
An exact price tie, which the authors consider improbable, is broken by time; therefore front-running remains possible there~\cite{src09moosavi2023}. % [09] p. 11 (F8)
Lissy has no order cancellation, because cancellations can be displaced until after the call is processed~\cite{src09moosavi2023}. % [09] p. 11 (F8)
Visible order cancellations on a continuous book, on the other hand, can be front-run~\cite{src02eskandari2019}, and our engine keeps cancellation. % [02] p. 6 (F4); OB:89-99
Lissy pays for this in throughput: in the worst case, one market close takes an entire block~\cite{src09moosavi2023}. % [09] p. 6 (F4)
Budish et al.\ suggest that the continuous limit order book is to blame for the speed race and propose using batch auctions, where a speed advantage would be worth only a fraction of its value in the continuous case~\cite{src11budish2015}. % [11] Sec. 1, Prop. 8 (F1, F6), no pages; EM M5a
Their evidence is derived from futures and equity markets, not a blockchain.

\emph{Defenses against reordering.}
Heimbach and Wattenhofer compare seven classes of defense mechanism, with seven criteria of evaluation: decentralization, security, scope, jostling, goodput, delay and cost; none meets all seven criteria~\cite{src18heimbach2022}. % [18] p. 5, p. 11 (F2, F5); EM M11a
Regarding qualitative assessment, on-chain commit and reveal performs well with respect to scope, jostling and decentralization, but poorly with respect to cost and delay, and an attacker can simply not reveal the order~\cite{src18heimbach2022}. % [18] p. 10 to 11 (F6); EM M4c
Canidio and Danos show that on-chain commit and reveal also deters the most harmful front-running for one extra message and a one-block delay, when guessing the hidden order is hard (their Proposition 2); their model excludes contracts that resolve competing claims, such as auctions~\cite{src20canidio2024}. % [20] p. 16, p. 27 to 28 (F5, F6, F10); EM M4a
In a price-time book with a predictable number of orders, an attacker can commit several orders and reveal only the more profitable ones~\cite{src02eskandari2019}. % [02] p. 15 (F8); EM M4b
Every deterministic sequencing rule has some instances where an AMM block producer makes risk-free profits~\cite{src21ferreira2023}. % [21] p. 4, p. 17, p. 25 (F4, F5); EM M11c
Encrypting pending transactions is another defense: Choudhuri et al.\ measure the decryption latency of their own scheme and report a flaw, since fixed, in a deployed one~\cite{src29choudhuri2024}. % [29] p. 6 to 7, p. 12 (F1, F5); EM M11d
None of these defenses changes the matching rule, so we believe no defense used individually is sufficient to address a one-transaction attack. % reasoning from EV E9 atomic

\subsection{The Gap}\label{sec:gap}
We found no measurement of front running a smart contract based limit order book, and no research on stop hunting through reference price manipulation in the crypto markets. % notes/PHASE4_GAPS.md gaps a, b
The deployed order book DEXs whose trading was measured in the works we read kept their order books off-chain~\cite{src12victor2021}. % [12] p. 2 (F1)
The attacks we analyze are therefore based on our implementation and design choices, and not on any real world examples; the measured sandwich attacks are on AMMs~\cite{src05qin2022,src04zhou2021}. % [05] p. 4; [04] p. 1
