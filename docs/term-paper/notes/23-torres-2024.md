## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- All detected MEV is AMM arbitrage (Uniswap V2/V3, Balancer, Curve) and lending liquidations (Aave, Compound) (p. 4, Sec. 3.1; p. 5, Sec. 3.2); no order-book DEX is measured.
- Measured only on Arbitrum, Optimism, zkSync up to 31 Aug 2023 (p. 7, Table 2); Base and Blast not measured (p. 12, Sec. 6.1).
- Detection is a lower bound (hard-coded events, CoinGecko prices) (p. 6 to 7, Sec. 3.7).
- Cross-layer sandwiches depend on L1-to-L2 message bridges (Hop Protocol victims) (p. 11, Sec. 5.3.1), not on in-contract visible orders.
- Sequencer assumed trusted and non-colluding (p. 9, Sec. 5.1).

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "MEV is widespread on rollups without a public mempool."
- Verdict: supported
- Evidence:
  - "MEV is widespread on rollups": holds. Abstract states MEV is widespread on rollups with trading volume comparable to Ethereum (p. 1); conclusion opens "MEV is omnipresent, even on rollups" (p. 13, Sec. 8). Rollups named: Arbitrum, Optimism, zkSync (p. 2, Sec. 1). Numbers: Arbitrum 7.2x more arbitrages than Ethereum in April 2023 (p. 7, Sec. 4.1); total MEV profit 18,275,617.54 USD Arbitrum, 3,822,404.73 USD Optimism, 650,976.49 USD zkSync (p. 7, Table 3).
  - "without a public mempool": holds as a fact about the setting: rollups "lack a public mempool like Ethereum, making the extraction of MEV more challenging" (p. 1) and Table 1 lists all studied rollups as "Private" mempool (p. 3). Nuance: the paper does not claim the absence of a public mempool causes MEV; it says it makes extraction harder, removes classic sandwiching (no sandwiches found, p. 7), and lowers profits (Ethereum profit close to 10x the three rollups combined, p. 7, Sec. 4.2). Wide MEV persists in spite of, not because of, the private mempool.
  - Caveat on "widespread": profits are much smaller than on Ethereum (p. 7, Sec. 4.2), and volume claim is in number of detected arbitrages, not USD.
