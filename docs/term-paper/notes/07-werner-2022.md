## 2. Findings
| # | Finding (own words) | Pointer (p. N, Sec.) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central contribution: split DeFi security into technical security (has a healthy literature) and economic security (largely unexplored). | p. 1 (Abstract, Sec. 1) |
| 2 | The split is by atomicity: technical exploits are atomic (single transaction or an ordered bundle, risk-free, outcome is profit or revert); economic exploits are strictly non-atomic, carry upfront cost, failure risk and market-response risk. | p. 1 (Sec. 1); p. 6 (Sec. 5); p. 8 to 9 (Sec. 6) |
| 3 | Transaction ordering attacks (displacement, multi-transaction sandwich, front- and back-running in one block) are classed as technical, risk-free for a miner or bundle submitter; the paper explicitly says sandwich attacks have been mislabelled as "economic risk". | p. 1 (Sec. 1); p. 7 to 8 (Sec. 5.3) |
| 4 | Economic risks "cannot be solved by technical means alone" (e.g., a time delay); defences need economic models, which "are sparsely studied". | p. 9 (Sec. 6) |
| 5 | Market manipulation even without instantaneous AMM prices: cost of moving a price over time, in an AMM or "through filling unfilled orders in an order book", is higher in thick markets and lower in thin ones; Inverse Finance and the DAI 1.30 USD Coinbase print (about 89m USD of liquidations) are examples. | p. 11 (Sec. 6.4) |
| 6 | Keepers: protocols that need state updates must rely on external, financially incentivised keepers because contracts cannot create transactions; liquidations can become unprofitable for keepers in illiquid crises. | p. 2 (Sec. 2.2); p. 10 (Sec. 6.1) |
| 7 | MEV threats (undercutting, time-bandit) are a consensus-layer risk; the miner's MEV ordering problem is conjectured NP-hard. | p. 10 (Sec. 6.2); p. 12 (Sec. 7.4) |
| 8 | On Clockwork Finance: its "economic security" is "closer to what we refer to as technical security or atomic MEV". | p. 12 (Sec. 7.4) |
| 9 | Privacy of DeFi is "a significantly understudied area"; Ethereum users have at best pseudonymity and privacy tools are costly. | p. 13 (Sec. 7.6) |
| 10 | Order-book DEXs exist (individual and batch settlement), but the paper focuses on AMMs; batch settlement (Gnosis) relies on competing keepers and can fail if the keeper market is not competitive or solution selection can be gamed. | p. 3 (Sec. 3.1); p. 17 (App. C) |

## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- Focus is on AMMs; order-book DEXs are mentioned only in passing (p. 3, Sec. 3.1; p. 17, App. C).
- Systematisation, not measurement: no new empirical data on ordering attacks.
- Technical vs economic boundary uses miner/bundle atomicity, a PoW-era Ethereum view (p. 6, Sec. 5).
- Exploit table covers February 2020 to March 2021 (p. 8, Table 1), before most order-book or L2 DEX activity.
- Some text post-dates the original preprint (e.g., Venus exploit "since pre-printing", p. 17, App. B), so version matters when quoting.

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "separates technical from economic security; the latter is less studied."
- Verdict: supported
- Evidence: Abstract: "We provide a distinction between technical security, which has a healthy literature, and economic security, which is largely unexplored" (p. 1). Sec. 1 repeats that "economic security risks were largely unexplored" before this paper (p. 1). Sec. 6: economic models "are sparsely studied" and defensive measures "are also not as well established" (p. 9). Nuance for the paper: the separation is by atomicity, and under this definition sandwich and other in-block ordering attacks are technical, not economic (p. 1, Sec. 1; p. 7 to 8, Sec. 5.3). The task gave no venue; the arXiv v6 PDF names none, and AFT 2022 could not be verified (see Sec. 1).
