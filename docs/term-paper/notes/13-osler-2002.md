## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- TradFi OTC currency market (dealer-monitored stops), 1996 to 2000 data; not a blockchain, not an exchange order book.
- Stops in the paper are private to the client and dealer (p. 9); on our chain every stop and trigger is public. Opposite information setting.
- Triggers in FX are dealer judgement on market rate; ours fire on lastTradePrice of any fill, even one lot. Paper says nothing about a one-lot or self-trade trigger.
- Deliberate triggering is lore only (p. 3); no cost or profit model for an attacker.
- Uses quotes, not transaction prices (p. 8); round-number effect depends on FX quotation conventions (p. 7, fn. 10). A token with 1e9 tick has its own "round numbers".
- Causality not established (p. 20, Sec. V-A).

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "clustered stop-loss orders contribute to price cascades."
- Verdict: supported (with the paper's own caveats)
- Evidence: Abstract p. 1 states three results that give empirical support to the hypothesis that stop-loss orders contribute to price cascades. Clustering: documented on p. 7 and Table III p. 31 (clustering data come from Osler (2002), a companion paper, summarised here). Cascade evidence: faster trends after crossing round numbers (pp. 11 to 12, Table V) and larger, longer responses than take-profits (pp. 16 to 18). Caveats: most results significant for hours but not days (p. 1); the statistics cannot prove causality (p. 5); stops are not claimed to be the only or primary cause of large moves (p. 6). Clustering data (1999 to 2000) and price data (1996 to 1998) are different periods and sources.
