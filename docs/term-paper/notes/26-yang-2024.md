## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- Empirical part is Ethereum L1 mainnet, 2022 (pre- and just post-Merge), PoW uncle-block effects included; not a rollup.
- All sandwich data is AMM-based (EigenPhi labels); nothing on limit order books.
- Countermeasure discussion is qualitative; no gas, latency, or throughput cost for commit and reveal, threshold encryption, or batch auctions.
- Published workshop version (10 pages) may not contain Section 4's numbers; I read only the arXiv v2.

## 5. Gap question answered
- Question(s): d, e.
- Answer from this source: d: names CoWSwap and FairTraDEX as batch auction DEXs and argues uniform price removes intra-batch ordering advantage (p. 7, Sec. 3.4), but gives no measured MEV reduction for any batch auction. e: names Shutter, Sikka, Osmosis (threshold encryption), TEX (timelock), Tesseract (TEE) and explains Shutter's epoch-key flow (p. 6, Sec. 3.3.1); states trust assumptions and residual leaks (metadata, content-agnostic frontrunning, p. 2 and Table 2 p. 8) but gives no measured costs. Only measured numbers concern MEV auction platforms (94.04% of sandwiches private, p. 9 Table 4; 8 private-victim sandwiches, p. 10; 68% longer wait for sanctioned transactions, p. 13).
- Strength: weak for d and e (peer-reviewed workshop SoK, but answers are argued not measured, and not about on-chain order books); moderate as a citable taxonomy that places M4, M5, M11.
