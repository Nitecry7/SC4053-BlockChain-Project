## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- Measured on Ethereum L1 mainnet in the PoW era (2017 to early 2019); PGA model assumes exponentially distributed block times (p. 10, Sec. V-A d), and the authors say sealed-bid auctions are more likely on PoS (p. 11, Sec. V-C c).
- Order-book DEXes studied kept the book off-chain (p. 3, Sec. II-D); no fully on-chain matching engine like ours is measured.
- Focus is pure revenue atomic arbitrage only (p. 4, Sec. III-A); sandwiching of limit orders, stop hunts and fill-cap effects are not studied.
- Time-bandit and undercutting attacks are consensus attacks; they do not map to contract-level fixes in our DEX.
- PGA mechanics (gas replacement, Parity 12.5% bump) are pre-EIP-1559 and pre-PBS; this paper predates private order flow (not discussed in the file).

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "bots compete in priority gas auctions for block position; introduces MEV; ordering dependence is a systemic risk."
- Verdict: supported
- Evidence:
  - "bots compete in priority gas auctions for block position": holds. Abstract says bots competitively bid up fees to obtain priority ordering, glossed as early block position and execution (p. 1); mechanism p. 5 to 6 (Sec. III-C).
  - "introduces MEV": holds. The introduction states that the paper introduces the notion of MEV (p. 2, Sec. I); defined p. 14 (Sec. VII). Note v1 spells it both with and without a hyphen (miner extractable value, miner-extractable value); the published subtitle uses Miner Extractable Value.
  - "ordering dependence is a systemic risk": holds with a nuance. The abstract states that high fees paid for priority ordering pose a systemic risk to consensus-layer security (p. 1), and the conclusion says OO fees threaten consensus stability (p. 17, Sec. IX). The systemic risk the paper means is consensus-layer (forking, time-bandit attacks), not risk to individual users. The abstract separately highlights large, complex risks created by transaction-ordering dependencies in smart contracts (p. 1).
  - Numbers if cited: lower bound over USD 6M to date (p. 2); 3.6% of blocks, 6.3% since block 7,000,000 (p. 15). These are from arXiv v1; not checked against the S&P text.
