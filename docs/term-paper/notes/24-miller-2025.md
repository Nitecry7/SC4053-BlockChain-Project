## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- Grey literature, not peer reviewed; the author's company sells the proposed solution ("Toward the solution").
- Measures AMM backrunning arbitrage spam only (DEX price queries to Uniswap-style pools); liquidations and other MEV excluded ("Appendix: Spam identification heuristics").
- Heuristic is easily gamed (author says so) ("Appendix").
- Only OP-Stack rollups measured (Base, OP Mainnet, Unichain, World mentioned); Solana, zkSync, Arbitrum claims rest on third parties, not shown ("Findings").
- Exact time windows are loose ("a million blocks in February", "November 2024 to February 2025"); no year stated for the February Base figure in the text read.
- Nothing about order books, stop orders, or fill caps.

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "spam bots use over half the gas on several rollups while paying under a tenth of fees."
- Verdict: partly supported
- Evidence:
  - "over half the gas on several rollups": holds. Intro: "Spam bots across multiple rollups are consuming more than 50% of gas and paying less than 10% of fees"; Sec. "1 Spam is systemic and widespread": Unichain, Base and OP Mainnet spam "regularly consumed over 50% of all gas used".
  - "paying under a tenth of fees": holds as stated in the intro bullet, but the per-chain numbers shown only partly back it: OP Mainnet ~57% of gas and ~9% of fees (under a tenth, yes); Base ~56% of gas and 14% of fees (NOT under a tenth) (Sec. "2 Spam consumes ..."). No fee share figure for Unichain found in the text. So "under a tenth" is shown for OP Mainnet only.
  - "several rollups": all OP-Stack (Unichain, Base, OP Mainnet). Non-OP rollups are mentioned only via others' findings (Sec. "Findings").
  - Must be labelled grey literature (Flashbots blog by Robert Miller, 16 Jun 2025), heuristic-based.
