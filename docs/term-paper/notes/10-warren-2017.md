## 2. Findings
| # | Finding (own words) | Pointer (p. N, Sec.) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Earlier Ethereum DEXs that keep the order book on-chain failed to get volume because makers pay gas every time they post, modify or cancel; frequent updates are "prohibitively expensive". On-chain books also consume bandwidth and bloat the chain without value transfer. No gas figure for on-chain books is given. | p. 4 (Sec. 2) |
| 2 | AMMs are presented as the alternative to on-chain books: always available, easy to integrate, but price impact is insensitive to liquidity (artificial supply curve). State channels need both parties online and are inefficient for one-time trades. | p. 4 (Sec. 2) |
| 3 | Proposed design: "off-chain order relay with on-chain settlement": maker signs an order off-chain (ECDSA over a Keccak hash), anyone relays it, a taker submits it to the exchange contract, which checks signature, expiry and fill state and swaps tokens via ERC20 approvals (no escrow deposit). | p. 4 (Sec. 2), p. 5 (Sec. 3, Fig. 2), p. 6 (Sec. 3.1) |
| 4 | The contract has two functions (fill, cancel), about 100 lines, and "costs approximately 90k gas to fill an order". Filled amounts are stored per order hash in a mapping, allowing partial fills (valueFill). | p. 9 (Sec. 3.2, 3.2.2) |
| 5 | Relayers host off-chain order books and earn fees (feeA, feeB, feeRecipient) but do not execute trades; takers execute their own trades, so the protocol "cannot support true market orders". | p. 7 to 8 (Sec. 3.1.2, Fig. 3, Table 2) |
| 6 | Cancellation is on-chain and costs gas, so it is a fallback; makers should rely on expiration. A cancel and a fill sent at about the same time race: one fails depending on mining order, and this uncertainty grows with a transaction backlog. Expiry is checked against the block timestamp at execution, not at broadcast. | p. 10 (Sec. 3.2.3, 3.2.4) |
| 7 | A Token Registry with stakeholder oversight is planned to let users verify token addresses and decimals, but the protocol can trade "any token that uses the ERC20 token interface". Future plan to identify tokens by three-character symbols. | p. 12 (Sec. 4.1.2) |
| 8 | Upgrades through a Proxy contract holding user approvals and a DAO (initially a multisig) that whitelists new exchange contracts; the paper notes such abstraction can create security risks for user funds. | p. 11 to 12 (Sec. 4.1.1, Fig. 4) |

## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- 0x keeps the book off-chain; only settlement is on-chain. Its cost argument is about a different architecture from ours.
- No measurements: "prohibitively expensive" has no gas number for on-chain books; the 90k gas fill figure is a 2017 estimate for a v1 contract.
- White paper, not peer reviewed; 2017 PoW Ethereum.
- No discussion of front-running beyond the cancel/fill race; no stops, no matching engine on-chain.
- Tokens are moved via approvals at fill time (no escrow), unlike our escrow model.

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "on-chain order books are costly; proposes off-chain relay with on-chain settlement."
- Verdict: supported
- Evidence: p. 4 (Sec. 2) says on-chain books require makers "to spend gas each time they post, modify or cancel an order" and that frequent modification "is prohibitively expensive"; no gas numbers are given for that claim. p. 4 to 5 (Sec. 2 and 3, Fig. 2) describe "off-chain order relay with on-chain settlement" as the adopted hybrid approach, extended so anyone can act as the exchange (relayer). Nuance: the paper credits the hybrid approach to earlier work (refs [15, 16], IDEX and EtherDelta) and says 0x extends it (p. 4).

## 6. One quote (optional, max one sentence)
> "While the cost of a single transaction is small, frequently modifying orders in response to evolving market conditions is prohibitively expensive." (p. 4, Sec. 2)
