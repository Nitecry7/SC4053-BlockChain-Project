## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- Incident data covers Ethereum and BSC only, sourced from Rekt News, Slowmist and Cryptosec, which do not document their collection process (sampling bias acknowledged) (p. 5 and 7, Sec. III).
- MEV/BEV incidents are only included if those three sources disclosed them (p. 7, footnote 17), so ordering attacks like C1 to C3 are likely undercounted.
- Most named incidents are AMM, lending, yield farming and bridge protocols; DEX losses were 450 million (12%) with no breakdown for order-book DEXs (p. 8, Table IV).
- Losses are reported values, cross-validated on-chain only "when applicable" (p. 5, Sec. III).
- Academic paper sample is limited to eight venues from 2018 to 2021 (p. 5, Sec. III).

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "77 papers, 30 audit reports, 181 incidents, at least 3.24 billion USD lost; oracle attacks under-studied."
- Verdict: partly supported
- Evidence:
  - "77 papers": holds as printed, "77 academic papers" (p. 1, Abstract; p. 13, Sec. VIII). Caveat: the Sec. III breakdown is 7 surveys/SoKs + 29 security tools + 42 attack papers = 78 (p. 5). Cite "77 academic papers" as the paper's headline figure.
  - "30 audit reports": holds, "30 recent public audit reports from 6 security testing companies" (p. 5, Sec. III).
  - "181 incidents": holds, "181 real-world incidents" (p. 1); Sec. III states 117 Ethereum and 69 BSC "(in total 181 incidents)" (p. 5), which sums to 186 without explanation in the text.
  - "at least 3.24 billion USD lost": holds, "a total loss of at least 3.24 billion USD from Apr 30, 2018 to Apr 30, 2022" (p. 1, Abstract). The summary omits the time window (four years, Apr 30, 2018 to Apr 30, 2022) and scope (Ethereum and BNB Smart Chain only, p. 1 Sec. I and p. 5 Sec. III); losses fall on users, liquidity providers, speculators and operators.
  - "oracle attacks under-studied": partly. The paper says "few academic papers address price oracle attacks" while on-chain oracle manipulation is the most frequent incident type at 15% (p. 1, Abstract; p. 12, Insight 3; Table III row 28(15%) incidents vs 4(9%) papers). It does not use the word "understudied" for oracles; that word is used for NET and CON incidents (p. 11, Insight 1). Also the 15% is "on-chain oracle manipulation" (PRO layer), not all oracle attacks; off-chain oracle manipulation is a separate AUX row. Safer wording: "few academic papers address price oracle attacks, although on-chain oracle manipulation is the most frequent incident type (28 incidents, 15%)".
