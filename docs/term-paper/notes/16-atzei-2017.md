## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- PoW-era Ethereum (2016 to 2017), Solidity 0.3.1 examples (p. 3, fn. 1); several issues fixed since (stack size attack impossible after 18 Oct 2016, p. 11 and p. 17 fn. 20; send warnings from v0.4.2, p. 15 fn. 15).
- Focus on technical bugs in single contracts; no market-design or MEV analysis, no order book.
- Examples use Ether transfers, not ERC20 tokens.
- No quantitative measurements of its own (cites ~28% unchecked send from other work, p. 8 and p. 20).

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "taxonomy of Ethereum contract vulnerabilities."
- Verdict: supported
- Evidence: Sec. 3 (p. 6, printed 169) systematizes the security vulnerabilities of Ethereum smart contracts into three levels (Solidity, EVM bytecode, blockchain) with 12 causes, each linked to attacks in Sec. 4. Abstract (p. 1) describes it as a taxonomy of common programming pitfalls that may lead to vulnerabilities. Precise wording: it is a taxonomy of causes of vulnerabilities (pitfalls), plus a set of attacks; claimed scope is all major vulnerabilities and attacks reported so far (p. 19), as of 2017.
