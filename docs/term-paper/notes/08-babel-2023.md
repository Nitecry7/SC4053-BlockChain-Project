## 2. Findings
| # | Finding (own words) | Pointer (p. N, Sec.) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduces extractable value (EV) as "the first formal definition for the economic security of composed smart contracts"; EV generalises MEV. | p. 5 (Sec. 1); p. 1 (Abstract) |
| 2 | EV is the maximum value, in the primary token, a player can extract from any valid sequence of blocks extending the current state; k-MEV is EV over k miner-built blocks. | p. 11 (Sec. 3) |
| 3 | EV is a local maximum for the current state only; a global maximum would need the distribution of all future transactions, which the authors call unrealistic. | p. 11 (Sec. 3, Remark 1) |
| 4 | MEV subsumes front-running, back-running, sandwiches and flash-loan attacks that can be run from the current state; non-profit exploits are out of scope ("traditional exploits"). | p. 12 (Sec. 3, Remark 2) |
| 5 | Composability is defined economically: a contract is epsilon-composable if adding it raises a player's MEV by at most a factor (1 + epsilon). | p. 16 (Sec. 4, Def. 1) |
| 6 | Composition properties: secure composition is state dependent, depends on the actions allowed (reorder only vs insert), a contract may not compose with another copy of itself, and adversarial contracts can break composability cheaply. | p. 16 (Sec. 4.1, Characteristics 1 to 4) |
| 7 | Reordering and insertion around a price source lets a miner trigger a betting payout or a Maker liquidation, and "also allows P to capitalize on the price differential between limit orders and market orders". | p. 18 (Sec. 4.2) |
| 8 | Exhaustive search over orderings is O(t!) in the number of transactions; blocks with 10 or more relevant AMM transactions were not fully explored (randomised search over 400,000 paths). | p. 21 (Sec. 5.1); p. 32 (Sec. 6.3) |
| 9 | Empirical: sampled MEV across the three AMMs extrapolates to about 56 million USD per month in 2021; Maker composed with a hypothetical Uniswap V2 oracle gives 542,827 USD of MEV over the top 20 liquidation blocks, "orders of magnitude larger than the block rewards". | p. 31 (Sec. 6.3); p. 35 (Sec. 6.4) |
| 10 | Developer takeaway: a contract's economic security may depend on other contracts, including ones deployed later; flash loans remove capital limits. | p. 20 (Sec. 4.5) |

## 4. Limits for an on-chain order book
- Models only Uniswap V1, Uniswap V2, Sushiswap and MakerDAO (plus toy flash loan, airdrop, vault, governance and betting models); no order book is modelled (p. 1, Abstract; p. 6, Sec. 1; p. 35 to 36, Sec. 6.5).
- Maker plus Uniswap oracle composition is hypothetical; MakerDAO does not use Uniswap as an oracle (p. 34, Sec. 6.4).
- Miner-centric PoW framing (block reward comparisons, k consecutive blocks); proposer-builder separation not covered (p. 11, Sec. 3; p. 41, App. B).
- EV ignores miner costs and is a local per-state maximum (p. 11, Remark 1; p. 41, App. B).
- Terminology: Werner et al. (07, p. 12, Sec. 7.4) say this paper's "economic security" is closer to their "technical security or atomic MEV"; define the term before using both sources.

## 5. Claim check
- Summary given: "extractable value as a formal measure of economic security under composition."
- Verdict: supported
- Evidence: p. 5 (Sec. 1): "we introduce in this paper, the first formal definition for the economic security of composed smart contracts, which we call extractable value (EV)". Abstract (p. 1) calls EV "a new formal notion of economic security in composed DeFi contracts". Composition is formalised as epsilon-composability over MEV (p. 16, Def. 1). Nuances: EV is a per-state local maximum (p. 11, Remark 1); composability uses k-MEV, a special case of EV, in Def. 1; the definition is miner/orderer-centric and covers profit-seeking attacks only (p. 12, Remark 2). "Economic security" here differs from Werner et al.'s non-atomic definition (07).
